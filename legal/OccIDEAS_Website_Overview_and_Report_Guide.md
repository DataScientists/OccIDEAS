# OccIDEAS: website overview and assessment report guide

Draft for review • 25 September 2026

## 1. Website overview

### Understanding your OccIDEAS exposure assessment

OccIDEAS estimates occupational exposure to hazardous agents using your answers to questions about the work you do, the materials you handle, your working environment and the protective measures you use.

The system applies peer-reviewed assessment rules developed and validated by experts in occupational epidemiology and occupational hygiene. These rules translate descriptions of everyday work activities into structured exposure assessments.

### What the assessment tells you

Your result is an estimate based on the information you provide. It is not a direct measurement of your exposure or a test of your health. Its relevance depends on how accurately and completely your answers describe your work during the period covered by the questionnaire.

Where the assessment uses the exposure-limit categories described below, the classifications mean:

| Classification | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| High | Your reported activities indicate estimated exposure above the exposure limit used by the assessment rule. |
| Medium | Your reported activities indicate estimated exposure below that limit, but at a level that may still warrant attention. |
| Low | Your reported activities indicate estimated exposure well below that limit, but exposure has not been classified as absent. |

The relevant agent and the basis for its classification are explained in your report. These categories describe estimated exposure, not the severity of an illness or your likelihood of developing one. A result below an exposure limit is not a guarantee that exposure is harmless.

### Exposure assessment and your health

OccIDEAS assesses occupational exposure. It does not assess your health, diagnose a medical condition, determine whether exposure has caused harm, or prescribe treatment.

Where an explanatory rationale is provided, it describes the answers and assessment rule supporting your result. Any information about an agent's health effects is general background information; it is not a conclusion about your individual health.

For a High result, the report recommends seeking advice from a qualified health service provider. That provider can consider your exposure assessment alongside your medical history and other relevant information, and decide whether any health assessment or follow-up is appropriate. The recommendation does not mean that an illness or injury has been identified.

### Participant identity in the study arrangement

In studies using this arrangement, OccIDEAS stores questionnaire responses and exposure results against a participant ID. The study organisation retains the information linking that ID to the participant's identity and does not provide that linking information to OccIDEAS. Results are exported to the study organisation, which manages any communication with participants.

Refer to the study's participant information and privacy notice for details of how your information is handled and whom to contact with questions.

## 2. Standard wording for assessment reports

### Opening statement

This report presents an occupational exposure assessment based on your questionnaire responses for [period]. OccIDEAS has applied peer-reviewed expert rules to those responses to estimate exposure to the agents covered by this assessment. The results are estimates, not direct exposure measurements. This report does not assess your health or determine whether exposure has caused harm.

### High exposure result template

**Agent:** [agent name]

**Estimated exposure classification:** High

**Answers supporting this result:** [List the relevant reported tasks, materials, working conditions and protective measures accurately.]

**Why this classification was assigned:** Based on these answers, the applicable OccIDEAS rule estimates exposure above [identify the exposure limit or benchmark used, including the relevant averaging period where applicable]. This is a questionnaire-based estimate; it does not establish a measured exceedance at your workplace.

**General information about the agent, if included:** [Insert expert-reviewed, agent-specific background information. Keep general hazard information separate from the individual's estimated exposure result.]

**What this means for your health:** This result does not establish whether your health has been affected. Seek advice from a qualified health service provider and share this report so they can consider whether a health assessment or follow-up is appropriate.

### Medium and Low result wording

**Medium:** Based on your reported activities, the applicable rule estimates exposure below [benchmark], but at a level that may still warrant attention. This classification does not establish that exposure is harmless or assess your health.

**Low:** Based on your reported activities, the applicable rule estimates exposure well below [benchmark], but does not classify exposure as absent. This classification does not establish that exposure is harmless or assess your health.

Do not use either template for an incomplete or unassessable result. Where information is insufficient, state the limitation rather than assigning reassuring language unsupported by the rule.

## 3. Report alignment checklist — internal use

| Check | Required alignment |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | Describe an occupational exposure estimate. Do not describe a health assessment, diagnosis, personal disease prediction or treatment recommendation. |
| Evidence | Connect the result to the answers actually supplied. Do not add assumptions about tasks, duration or protective measures without identifying them. |
| Rule provenance | Use the approved rule and retain its identifier/version for traceability. Distinguish validation of the rule from review of the explanatory text. |
| Category meaning | Ensure High, Medium and Low match the approved rule definitions. Do not interpret these levels as confidence or probability unless the rule separately defines such a dimension. |
| Benchmark | Confirm the benchmark, jurisdiction, version, units and averaging period where applicable. Do not automatically describe every agent's benchmark as a statutory limit. |
| Exceptions | Where an agent has a different assessment basis, use its approved definition rather than forcing it into the website's limit-based explanation. |
| Measurement | Say “estimated exposure” or “the rule estimates”. Do not claim a measured concentration, confirmed exceedance or legal breach from questionnaire results alone. |
| Health effects | Keep general hazard information separate from conclusions about the participant. Do not infer illness, injury or individual disease risk. |
| Follow-up | Recommend professional health advice for High results, without implying that harm has been found or that specific testing or treatment is necessary. |
| Lower results | Do not use “safe”, “harmless”, “nothing to worry about” or “no follow-up needed” solely because the classification is Medium or Low. |
| Missing data | Distinguish insufficient information, no identified exposure and Low exposure according to the approved rules. |
| Language | Use clear, factual wording. Avoid alarmist language and unsupported reassurance. |
| Identity | Keep the participant ID in OccIDEAS outputs; do not introduce names or contact details into the workflow described on the website. |
| Review | Have occupational exposure experts review the new rationales and category explanations before participant use. Check that website and report wording stay consistent when rules change. |

### Specific corrections to the supplied draft rationales

- Reconcile phrases such as “moderate-probability signal” and “low-probability or lower-level exposure” with the confirmed category definitions. Do not combine exposure probability and exposure level in one explanation unless the model explicitly supports both.
- Review statements such as “logged rather than flagged as a concern”: readers may interpret them as a health reassurance that the exposure classification alone cannot support.
- Replace wording about whether exposure “reaches a level of real concern” with the precise, approved interpretation of the applicable exposure benchmark.
- Verify agent-specific health-effect claims independently of the already validated exposure rules.

## 4. Publication notes — internal use

The description of expert development, peer review and rule validation comes from the OccIDEAS owner's explanation. This draft does not independently audit that evidence. Confirm that the wording covers the rules actually deployed in the relevant study.

The participant identity paragraph describes the arrangement discussed: the study organisation holds the identity link and receives an exported results file. Use it only for deployments that follow that arrangement. It is not a complete privacy notice.

This overview describes product function. It intentionally makes no categorical claim that OccIDEAS data is legally anonymous, that reports cannot be health information, or that the Privacy Act does not apply. Whether an activity is a health service, whether an output is health information, and whether someone is reasonably identifiable are separate legal questions. Confirm any legal classification through review of the actual service, final reports and data arrangements.

For airborne contaminants, Safe Work Australia explains that exposure standards do not mark a dividing line between healthy and unhealthy working environments. That is why this draft uses “exposure limit” rather than “safe limit” and avoids treating below-limit results as proof of safety. Check benchmark currency when rules or reports are updated.
